Areas of practice

Explore the clinic’s documented surgical areas. This website provides general educational information, not an individual diagnosis or treatment recommendation.

01

Rhinoplasty

Nose surgery may address nasal appearance, structure and, where clinically indicated, breathing concerns. Assessment includes nasal anatomy, functional findings, medical history and realistic expectations.

Learn more ↗
02

Revision rhinoplasty

Revision surgery is considered after a previous nasal operation when aesthetic or functional concerns persist. Scar tissue and altered anatomy can make planning more complex.

Learn more ↗
03

Breast augmentation

Breast augmentation may use implants or other techniques where clinically suitable to change breast volume and shape. Implant-related risks, follow-up and individual anatomy require careful discussion.

Learn more ↗
04

Breast reduction

Breast reduction removes breast tissue and skin to address selected functional or physical concerns. Planning considers symptoms, desired proportion, scars, sensation and recovery.

Learn more ↗
05

Tummy tuck (abdominoplasty)

Abdominoplasty may remove excess abdominal skin and, when appropriate, address abdominal wall laxity. It involves scars, recovery time and surgical risks that require detailed evaluation.

Learn more ↗
06

Eyelid surgery

Blepharoplasty can address selected excess skin or tissue around the eyelids. Eye health, dry-eye symptoms, eyelid function, scar placement and risks are important parts of assessment.

Learn more ↗
07

Gynecomastia surgery

Gynecomastia surgery addresses selected enlargement of male breast tissue. Assessment should distinguish glandular tissue from fat and investigate possible underlying causes before an operation.

Learn more ↗
08

Liposuction & body contouring

Liposuction is a body-contouring procedure for selected localized fat deposits; it is not a substitute for weight management. Skin elasticity, overall health and complication risks influence suitability.

Learn more ↗

Individual examination is essential before any surgical decision.
